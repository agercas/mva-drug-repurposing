# Readthrough and nonsense-mediated decay inhibition

## Claim as stated

Amlexanox, ataluren, aminoglycosides, ELX-02 or azithromycin restore full-length BubR1, the spindle checkpoint protein encoded by *BUB1B*, from the stop-gain allele (a variant that creates a premature stop codon). They act either by readthrough, in which the ribosome inserts an amino acid at the stop, or by inhibiting nonsense-mediated decay (NMD), the pathway that degrades messenger RNA carrying a premature stop. One proposal states that amlexanox was found by screening 1,200 drugs and "restores full-length functional protein". About eight public proposals include it and five consider and reject it.

## Evidence for

- Amlexanox was selected from a screen of more than 1,200 marketed drugs at 10 µM in a HeLa reporter of NMD (PMID [22938201](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22938201/); full text read). At up to 25 µM it raised nonsense-containing mRNA and gave full-length protein in patient-derived or cancer lines carrying nonsense mutations in dystrophin and the cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator (CFTR). In the p53-mutant Calu-6 line the full-length p53 product was "very low".

- Amlexanox gave full-length type VII collagen from 8 of 12 premature stop alleles in patient cells, in some cases above 50% of normal (PMID [28549954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28549954/); abstract read). It stabilised mRNA from a *GDAP1* stop allele in patient-derived neuronal cells at 100 µM for 20 hours (PMID [37513945](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37513945/); abstract read).

- Readthrough compounds have reached phase 2 and phase 3 trials for nonsense variants in other genes (ataluren in cystic fibrosis, NCT02139306; ELX-02 in nephropathic cystinosis, NCT04069260; ClinicalTrials.gov, read 2026-10-24).

## Evidence against

1. **No *BUB1B* allele was tested** in any cited paper.

2. **The screen used a reporter in HeLa cells in a single pass;** the paper's statement that the full-length proteins are functional rests on its own assays in other genes. The *COL7A1* (type VII collagen gene) paper (PMID [28549954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28549954/)) tested amlexanox on stop alleles in patient cells; the 1,200-drug screen comes from the other paper (PMID [22938201](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22938201/)).

3. **Readthrough inserts a near-cognate amino acid** (one whose transfer RNA nearly matches the stop codon) at the stop codon, which need not be the wild-type leucine.

4. **Truncated BubR1 protein was undetectable in patient cells,** and NMD is inferred from Northern blots in two experiments (PMID [20516114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20516114/); full text read, methods and figure legends).

5. **The dose needed is unmeasured.** The HeLa titration (about 13% of normal BubR1 harmless, about 6% harmful; PMID [20516114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20516114/)) suggests that a small gain in level may matter, and the residual level of the child's stop allele is unmeasured.

## Conclusion

Evidence comes from other genes, at 25 to 100 µM in the cell systems cited, with readthrough inferred. The approach is possible and unsupported for *BUB1B*.

## Same weakness in this report

No readthrough measurement exists at codon 737, the residue inserted there is unmeasured, and the stop-allele transcript is a decay target that NMD may already have cleared. A reporter assay (a construct whose signal reports readthrough) with peptide mass spectrometry decides the residue, and an allele-resolved transcript measurement decides the decay. The suppressor tRNA, which inserts leucine, is assessed in [sequence-level rescue routes](sequence-level-rescue-routes.md).
