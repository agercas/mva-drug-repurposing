# MEK inhibitors for clearing aneuploid cells

## Claim as stated

Trametinib and selumetinib block MEK (mitogen-activated protein kinase kinase), a step in the RAF/MEK/ERK signalling pathway. One proposal names trametinib as its defensible candidate on the grounds that human aneuploid cells depend on this pathway, so inhibition would remove aneuploid cells and lower tumour risk. A second proposal lists both drugs as options for the tumour setting.

## Evidence for

- In non-transformed RPE1-hTERT cells (human retinal pigment epithelial cells immortalised with telomerase), clones with induced aneuploidy showed higher RAF/MEK/ERK activity and more sensitivity to drugs against this pathway, most of all to CRAF inhibition. CRAF or MEK inhibition also sensitised them to DNA-damaging chemotherapy and to PARP inhibitors, and the authors validated the result in human cancer cell lines (PMID [39251587](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39251587/); abstract read).

- Selumetinib has a paediatric indication: safety and effectiveness are established from 1 year of age for neurofibromatosis type 1 with inoperable plexiform neurofibromas (label read). Trametinib is established from 1 year of age in combination with dabrafenib for BRAF V600E tumours (label read).

## Evidence against

1. **The mechanism is clearance.** Sensitivity of aneuploid cells to MEK inhibition removes those cells. In a constitutional mosaic the aneuploid cells are the child's own tissue, and restoring checkpoint function needs a different mechanism.

2. **The combination results use genotoxic agents.** Besides single-agent sensitivity, the paper shows MEK and CRAF inhibition sensitising aneuploid cells to DNA-damaging chemotherapy and PARP inhibitors, agents that fail the genotoxicity gate in section 3, so those results fall outside this report's screen (PMID [39251587](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39251587/); abstract read).

3. **The cited work uses RPE1 clones with induced aneuploidy and cancer cell lines.** No MEK inhibitor has been tested in a BubR1-deficient system or in a model of mosaic variegated aneuploidy.

4. **The selumetinib label records an aneugenic finding.** Selumetinib raised micronucleated immature erythrocytes in mouse micronucleus studies "predominantly via an aneugenic mode of action", at doses above 160 mg/kg, about 38 times the human maximum concentration at the clinical dose (label read). An aneugen is an agent that disturbs chromosome segregation.

5. **The trametinib label has gaps.** Carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted, and the safety and effectiveness of trametinib as a single agent in children are not established. The paediatric indication is the combination with dabrafenib (label read).

6. **The first proposal's own adversarial analysis states that trametinib should not be shortlisted.**

## Conclusion

The approach removes aneuploid cells in RPE1 and cancer-line models. Evidence that it restores function in a BubR1-deficient cell is absent, the selumetinib label records an aneugenic finding, and trametinib has no carcinogenicity study. The approach is unsupported for this disease.

## Same weakness in this report

No candidate in this report has been tested in a BubR1-deficient human cell. The approved-drug screen in section 5.10 carries a sign control that shows whether a compound raises or lowers errors in checkpoint-weak cells, and it would read a clearance agent.
