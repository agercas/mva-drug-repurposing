# Interferon blockade with JAK inhibitors and anifrolumab

## Claim as stated

Micronuclei (small nuclei that enclose missegregated chromosomes) and chromosome missegregation activate cytosolic DNA sensing by cGAS (cyclic GMP-AMP synthase) and STING (stimulator of interferon genes). This produces chronic interferon signalling that drives inflammation in mosaic variegated aneuploidy (MVA), a disorder of chromosome missegregation linked to *BUB1B*, the gene for the spindle checkpoint protein BubR1.

JAK (Janus kinase) inhibitors such as ruxolitinib, baricitinib or tofacitinib, an antibody to the type-I interferon receptor (anifrolumab), or blockade of interleukin-6 (IL-6) should therefore help. One proposal puts it forward only if an interferon or IL-6 signature is present, and another considers and rejects it.

## Evidence for

- Enhanced interferon and NF-κB (nuclear factor kappa B) signalling appeared in both aneuploid and euploid (normal chromosome number) blood cells of one adult with biallelic *MAD1L1* variants, which affect both copies of the gene (PMID [36322655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322655/); abstract and methods read).

- RPE-1 cells arrested after chromosome missegregation, with complex karyotypes, produce pro-inflammatory signals, and cGAS/STING pathway genes were upregulated in them (PMID [28633018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28633018/); full text read, Fig. S7G). The authors note that it is unclear whether this indicates cytoplasmic DNA.

- A 2023 review states that micronuclei are detected by cGAS, which triggers inflammatory signalling through STAT1, STAT3 and NF-κB, and that cancers with chromosomal instability circumvent this response (PMID [37561163](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37561163/); abstract read). The review summarises work in cancer cells and reports no new data.

- In human progeria (premature-ageing) fibroblasts, the farnesyltransferase inhibitor lonafarnib increased cytosolic DNA fragments and activated the cGAS-STING-STAT1 axis, and baricitinib prevented that activation (PMID [34299092](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34299092/); abstract read; in vitro, a drug-induced axis, one study).

- Trisomy 21 (Down syndrome, an extra copy of chromosome 21) activates the interferon response in fibroblasts, lymphoblastoid lines and circulating immune cells, which the authors attribute, as a likely cause, to the gene dosage of four interferon receptor genes on chromosome 21. JAK inhibition rescued the proliferation defect of trisomy 21 fibroblasts (PMID [27472900](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27472900/); abstract read).

- JAK inhibition with tofacitinib decreased interferon scores in the first 10 participants with Down syndrome to finish 16 weeks. The trial (NCT04246372) enrolled people aged 12 to 50 years with moderate-to-severe alopecia areata, hidradenitis suppurativa, psoriasis, atopic dermatitis or vitiligo, and no serious adverse events were reported (PMID [39737640](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39737640/); full text read; single-site open-label phase 2 interim analysis).

- Interferon signatures normalised in 6 of 7 children with Aicardi-Goutières syndrome treated with baricitinib or anifrolumab, and 6 of 7 stabilised or improved clinically (PMID [42033272](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42033272/); abstract read; retrospective, no control group, agent split not stated).

## Evidence against

1. **The human signature is from a different gene** (*MAD1L1*) in one adult, and cGAS/STING activity is unmeasured in that patient (PMID [36322655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36322655/)).

2. **No cGAS/STING or JAK intervention was run in any BubR1 or MVA model** in the papers read. Those papers report no interferon signature in a patient with *BUB1B* variants.

3. **The precedents come from other settings.** Progeria cells, Down syndrome and Aicardi-Goutières syndrome are interferon-high or gene-dosage diseases, and none is a chromosomal-instability syndrome with random aneuploidy. The Down syndrome trial population has autoimmune or inflammatory skin disease and one fixed extra chromosome, so transfer to random aneuploidy is inferred. The interim analysis (10 participants, 16 weeks) addresses interferon scores and early safety, and malignancy lies outside its scope.

4. **Public data could not test the premise.** In public Perturb-seq data (pooled gene perturbation with single-cell RNA readout) from HCT116 cells (X-Atlas/Orion, licensed CC BY-NC-SA; Huang et al. 2025, preprint DOI [10.1101/2025.06.11.659105](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.06.11.659105)), the positive controls for nucleic-acid sensing (*DNASE2*, *RNASEH2A*, *RNASEH2C*, *IFIH1*, *ZNFX1*) raised no interferon-stimulated-gene programme. The null in RPE1 and K562 cells (Replogle et al. 2022, PMID [35688146](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35688146/)) is uninformative, since both lines express almost no cGAS (0.0 and 0.2 normalised transcripts per million).

5. **Chronic activation can dampen itself.** In cancer cell lines and mouse models, chromosomal-instability-driven cGAS-STING activation produced type-I interferon tachyphylaxis (a falling response) downstream of STING, with endoplasmic-reticulum stress signalling and a pro-metastatic microenvironment (PMID [37612508](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37612508/); abstract read; summarised in PMID [38253764](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38253764/), full text read). The data come from tumour cells.

6. **JAK inhibition may reduce natural-killer clearance of aneuploid cells.** Natural-killer cells clear untransformed aneuploid senescent cells through NF-κB signalling in an in vitro killing system (PMID [34105235](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34105235/); abstract read). In 28 patients with myeloproliferative neoplasms, with or without ruxolitinib, compared with 24 healthy individuals, ruxolitinib reduced natural-killer cell number and killing (PMID [25832652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25832652/); abstract read).

   A short commentary by the same group states that cell numbers rose back to normal in patients who stopped the drug (PMID [26425655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26425655/); read in full, no abstract). Whether this raises tumour risk in a child with MVA is untested.

7. **The JAK inhibitor labels carry cancer-risk findings.** Tofacitinib and baricitinib carry a boxed warning for serious infections, mortality, malignancy, major adverse cardiovascular events and thrombosis. Their paediatric indications start at 2 years (tofacitinib, juvenile arthritis) and 12 years (baricitinib, alopecia areata). The oral ruxolitinib label has no boxed warning, and its paediatric indications start at 12 years for graft-versus-host disease.

8. **The anifrolumab label reports unresolved cancer findings.** Carcinogenic and genotoxic potential have not been evaluated, and increased carcinogenic potential was observed in rodent models of IFNAR1 blockade, of unknown clinical relevance. Paediatric safety and efficacy are not established.

9. **The child's interferon signature is unmeasured.**

## Conclusion

The approach is possible and unsupported. Its premise comes from a signal in another gene and from precedents in interferon-high and gene-dosage diseases, and the labels of the agents carry malignancy findings.

## Same weakness in this report

The interferon-stimulated-gene score of patient-derived cells has not been measured in MVA, and that measurement is the first step for any interferon-directed idea. The cytokine panel proposed for the anakinra card rests on the same inflammatory premise.
